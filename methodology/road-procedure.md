# Road and track procedure

## Claim
Without a dyno, the driver is the load control. Use smooth inputs and data logging, and only as much road risk as the task needs.

## Why (derivation)
Steady-state road tuning uses gear choice and the brakes for load; hard use heats brakes and temperatures drift. Full-power runs cannot be watched live safely, so they are logged and reviewed.

## Assumptions
A safe place to test and an extra person where possible.

## Breaks when
Road gradient, wind, traffic and legal limits.

## Practical use
Pick the gear for the speeds involved. Log, stop, review, then change. Never watch a laptop during a pull.

## Decision rules
Drive a few minutes before adjusting. Use smooth throttle changes to keep enrichment out of the data. Stop if brakes fade or smell hot, and let them cool by moving, not by stopping. Treat MBT as unknown without torque data: limit the timing to the knock margin approach. Road safety outranks data.

Do the ten-step process on a road car too, adapted to conditions. Accept 1 to 2 % from target; chasing a perfect lambda wastes time. Confirm any dyno tune in real driving.

## Data gates
Same as data-quality, plus road conditions noted.

## Worked example
A log shows a clean pull except where a bend forced a lift. Only the clean part is used.

## Disputed / conflicts
None recorded.
