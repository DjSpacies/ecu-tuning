# Dyno procedure

## Claim
A dyno gives controllable load and repeatable tests, which allows measured torque decisions, but the temperature state is the operator's job.

## Why (derivation)
Steady-state load lets cells be tested one by one; torque readings allow MBT decisions. A dyno cell often has poorer cooling airflow than the road, so temperature creeps.

## Assumptions
The vehicle is restrained and safety systems set up.

## Breaks when
Heat soak on low-airflow dynos makes runs not comparable.

## Practical use
Check coolant and air temperature after every change. Compare runs only when they match.

## Decision rules
Never touch the brake pedal during a dyno run. Check temperatures between runs. Longer ramps put more load on the engine and test thermal margin.

Start every run from the same temperatures. Tyre deformation on a rolling road makes high-torque data less consistent, so compare like with like. Stay at the cell centre during steady-state work.

## Data gates
Run-to-run temperature comparable. Torque repeatability better than the size of the change being judged.

## Worked example
Two pulls differ by 8 kW, but the second started 15 C hotter. The difference cannot be attributed to a tuning change.
